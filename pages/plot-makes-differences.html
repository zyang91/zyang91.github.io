Experiments Charts Three figures R · ggplot2

Plot makes differences.

All three could have been tables. Same numbers, same honesty, nobody reading past the first row.

So each comes with its receipts.

01

Ranked bars · Regional economies

Rust Belt cities still run on hospitals and universities

Anchor institutions are the places that cannot leave town. In Cleveland they are 14% of the economy. In San Jose, four.

Two sentences sit inside the panel, one by the teal, one by the navy. Between them you learn what the colors mean, without leaving the chart.

What the chart does

  • Sorted, so the ranking carries the eye.
  • Colored by region, four groups with a job to do.
  • A dashed line at 9% gives every bar something to sit against.
  • Numbers at the bar ends, so nobody measures.
02

Trend lines · Traffic speeds

Chicago drives faster, but every city crawls by rush hour

Average weekday speed, hour by hour. The shape is the same everywhere. Only the height changes.

The faint dots are the raw weekly numbers, left in so you can see how noisy the measurement is. The bold lines stay the thing you follow.

What the chart does

  • Names at the right edge instead of a legend.
  • A shaded band for rush hour, as background not data.
  • Faded points keep the spread honest.
  • Hours named, not numbered zero to twenty-three.
03

Scatter · Pedestrian safety

Where children walk in danger, everyone does

Child pedestrian deaths against everyone else’s, across 30 metros. Streets that kill children kill adults too.

Thirty labels would be a mess. Most points stay gray and nameless, here to make the cloud. Only the metros with something to say get a name, and McAllen gets one for breaking it.

What the chart does

  • Gray points still shape the trend line.
  • Curved leaders point without crossing data.
  • One thin fitted line, a reference and not a claim.
  • Arrows on the axis titles say which way is worse.

Why bother styling a chart?

Because the plain version is not neutral. It was styled too, by software, for nobody in particular.

A headline that says something. A sentence inside the panel. A source line at the bottom. None of it is hard. It is mostly deciding to treat a figure as writing.

The maps are on North Is a Choice. The rest is in Thoughts.
